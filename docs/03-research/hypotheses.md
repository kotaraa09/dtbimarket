# Hypotheses

Written before data collection. Add new hypotheses with a date; do not rewrite old ones to match results.

## H1 — framing affects action

The same recommendation, framed differently, produces different 7-day action rates.

*Reasoning:* these owners are students with limited time and no obligation to act. The cost of acting is identical across framings, so any difference is attributable to how the request was presented.

## H2 — comparison outperforms instruction

A recommendation framed as a peer comparison ("stores in your category have 4 photos per product; yours has 1") produces a higher action rate than the same recommendation framed as an instruction ("add 3 more product photos").

*Reasoning:* a comparison supplies a reason and a target. An instruction supplies neither.

## H3 — effect decays

Action rates fall over the study period regardless of framing, as novelty wears off.

*Reasoning:* if true, this constrains how much any advisory feature can be expected to achieve over a full semester, which is a finding in its own right.

---

**If H1 is not supported**, that is still a reportable result: it would suggest that for micro-entrepreneurs the barrier to acting is capacity rather than motivation, and that presentation is not where effort should be spent. Record it as a finding, not a failure.
